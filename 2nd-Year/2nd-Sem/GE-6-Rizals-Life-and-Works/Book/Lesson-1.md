# **LAW. LITERATURE. AND NATIONAL HEROES**

There could be no greater way to begin this narrative besides digging up the truth and controversy regarding the Republic Act 1425 (Iniego, 2015). The fact that a country needed a particular law to inculcate nationalism and patriotism is in itself the greatest controversy. The greater issue and confusion are actually the "failure" of such a law, because we still can observe many people wishing Rizal a Happy Birthday on his death anniversary\! Maybe this is a shallow basis, but this proves the point that the law has failed to live for its purpose. For many, the blame lies upon the superficial execution of the law, and perhaps they are right. But thè interesting angle that we need to look at is the rationale, including the controversies it endured before it became a law. Once the rationale, substance, and controversies surrounding the passing of the law are laid down, one can gain not only enough knowledge of the technicalities but also of feeling the spirit of the law.

# **RIZAL LAW: FACTS, CONTROVERSIES, AND PURPOSE**

On April 3, 1956, the Commission on Education filed the Senate Bill 438 drafted by the then-senator Claro M. Recto. That time, only three senators expressed their disapproval, a reason why they hoped this would be passed as a law smoothly. However, with the sponsorship of then-senator Jose P. Laurel, it became an object of hot debate which lasted for three weeks. The debate started at the Senate on April 23; on one faction were the proponents Laurel and Recto, together with Lorenzo Tañada who was later on recognized as the Grand Old Man of Philippine Politics because he had the longest term as a senator which lasted for 20 years; Quintin Paredes who was a mason and the first resident commissioner of the Commonwealth Republic; and Domacao Alonto, a nationalist and utmost promoter of Mindanao. On the other hand, also present were Senator Francisco Rodrigo, Decoroso Rosales, and Mariano Cuenco. The other senators also joined the debate, but the aforementioned are the primary persona. One of the highlights was the exchange between Recto and Rodrigo from which one always had a say against the other, almost never letting the other finish his statement.

The endorsement of the House Bill 5561 by Congressman Jacobo Gonzales on April 19, which was of almost similar version with the Senate Bill 438, also stirred the lower house. On May 9, an even hotter debate ensued, which almost ended up with Pampanga Congressman Emilio Cortes and Cebu Congressman Ramon Durano fist-fighting. Such bill was strictly opposed by Congresswoman Tecla San Andres Ziga, the first woman who topped the Bar exam; Congressman Ramon Durano, the patriarch of the Duranos of Cebu; Congressman Godofredo Ramos, known as the Father of Aklan and that after whom the Caticlan Airport at Boracay was named; and Congressman Miguel Cuenco, a Visayan lawyer and the brother of Senator Cuenco.

The hot debate in the Congress rippled out to the common society. There was the campaign of Bacolod Archbishop Manuel Yap against those in favor of the Rizal Bill. He even threatened about the foreclosure of the churches if ever the Rizal Bill becomes a law, to which Recto blatantly responded that the government should take charge, then, of running the parishes. Those who bravely went against the Church was the Catholic Mayor of Manila Arsenio Lacson who was said to have walked out of the Holy Mass the moment the presider read the pastoral letter regarding the unfavorable Rizal Law. Another influential person who favored the Rizal Law was President Emilio Aguinaldo.

Because of the never-ending debate in the Senate which in form also divided the nation, Sen. Laurel endorsed the amendment of Senate Bill 438, filling in its lapses. The apparent difference between this amended bill compared to the original was learning Rizal's other works and writings besides Noli Me Tangere and El Filibusterismo. For Laurel, we cannot find in the amended bill the rationale behind the compulsory reading of the unexpurgated copies \- copies that did not pass through any censorship \- in Rizal's novels. However, the unexpurgated copies were to be for the consumption of tertiary students, the very reason why Sen. Rodrigo wanted total abolition of such provision. But Laurel did not give his approval; thus Sen. Roseller Lim suggested the students be given exemption from reading the original text especially if reading such could ruin one's faith. However, it almost sparked another debate. Sen. Cipriano Primicias suggested a provision on the original text and that this not be removed from the learning materials in the subjects relating to Rizal.

In the lower house, the debate went on, until Congressman Arturo Tolentino, the then House Majority Floor Leader, endorsed the exact copy of the first amended bill of Laurel, as a substitute to the original Gonzales Bill. After ironing a few wrinkles, the third and final reading of the amended Gonzales Bill was approved. On the same day, the amended substitute Bill of Laurel was also approved. On that day, May 17, 1956, such a bill was passed in the Congress, signed by President Ramon Magsaysay on June 12, 1956, and was officially turned into a law which got published in the official gazette of the Republic of the Philippines.

But the question is, what was the reason behind the long-withstanding debate? What was the substance and content of that law?

Senate Bill 438 is the act that mandates Noli Me Tangere and El Filibusterismo be read and taught obligatorily in all public and private colleges and universities. If one is to read the content of the whole law, besides Section 4 that states, it is not prohibiting or limiting the studying and learning about other heroes, the rationale is primarily the reading of the two novels, including the penalty to those who shall not abide. Since it is almost identical with the House Bill endorsed by Congressman Gonzales, it is important to understand that both have differing substantial content.

In frank terms, the laws were in great favor of Rizal. Whatever the Section 4 of the Senate Bill states, the fact that it was mandatory to read about Rizal only shows bias. This, in itself, is already controversial, not only because he is put on a higher pedestal compared to the other heroes, but also because of the complicated relationship Rizal has with the Catholic Church, the country's dominant religion.

Out of all the works of Rizal, it was Noli Me Tangere and El Filibusterismo that were obligatory to read, the novels which, based upon the research they had, were not only anti-friar but also generally against the Catholic faith. In the 333 pages of Noli, only 25 speak of nationalism, while 120 speak against the church, and 170 speak against the Catholic faith, not including the 50 pages from El Filibusterismo.

That is why it is highly understandable how the Church, directly from its pastoral letters and through the Congress apologists, really went against the bill. It is also understandable why there was the persistent opposition of Senator Rodrigo about the provision that obliges the college students to read the unexpurgated copies of Noli and El Fili. To do so would violate the freedom of conscience and religion upheld by the 1935 Constitution. This is solely because the Catholic faith might be shaken, especially if not taught well. They suggested, therefore, that instead of Noli and Fili, why not create an anthology of Rizal's works instead, which could clearly portray his nationalism and patriotism.

It is clear then that it wasn't nationalism and patriotism that rooted the debate. The emphasis of Recto on nationalism cannot be denied. We can even say that the weight he gave the Filipino-First Policy was even deeper than that of Magsaysay's. This is contradictory to his principles and words. Senator Jose P. Laurel's nationalism was not likewise hindered despite being the president of the Puppet Government during the Japanese occupation. One of the things that can prove this was his integrity as part of Governor General Leonard Wood. This is because the latter was against the Filipinization of the government. Congressman Jacobo Gonzalės, on his end, a Sakdalista which means he was one among those who campaigned for immediate independence.

Those who led the opposition are those whose nationalism cannot be denied. Senator Francisco Rodrigo made a campaign for the Filipino Language. Raul Manglapus led the propaganda materials against the Japanese during the Japanese occupation. Sen. Docoroso Rosales was a member of the Japanese resistance in Cebu.

The difference between the two factions is that one is closer to the Catholic Church; Soc Rodrigo did not expect a place in the senate if it weren't for the Catholic vote. Sen. Decoroso Rosales was the brother of Julio Cardinal Rosales, Archbishop of Cebu. The third pioneer of the opposition, Senator Mariano Cuenco, was the brother of Archbishop Jose Maria Cuenco.

Come to think of it, there was no rivalry between both factions. Both of them only had differing principles to uphold. They were right when they emphasized how nationalism and patriotism must be observed. They were also right when they wanted to acknowledge Rizal for his life and writings by learning about them. But the opposition could also be right about saying that, if nationalism was the primary goal, why not learn only "Sa Aking mga Kababata" or "Mi Ultimo Adios"?

The deeper issue lay within the intervention of the Church. Those who were in favor of the Bill have reflected upon how even if we totally eradicate frailocracy Rizal would still be persecuted; ironically, the secular priests would be the first to persecute him.

Today, the argument thrown by those who wish the Church would never meddle with the affairs of the government is the Separation of the Church and the State, like what transpired in the RH Law only recently, and the issue between the Church and the current administration.

# **SENATE BILL 438 & HOUSE BILL 5561: BACKGROUND, RATIONALE, AND CONTENT**

After the United States bought the Philippines from Spain for $20 million USD under the Treaty of Paris, the Americans took over the colonization. The struggle for nationalism and independence ensued, by people like Antonio Luna and Macario Sakay. After many years, and through the dialogues between the American colonial government and the Filipino leaders, independence was gradually granted to the Philippines. This began with the Jones Law of 1916, carried on by the Tydings- McDuffie Act of 1934, until it was formally granted on June 4, 1946, during the term of President Manuel Roxas.

Yet, after all these, real independence and unity, including nationalism, remains to be elusive. Even if we became independent, the presence and influence of imperial America still remains.

One concrete example is the persistent battle of the HukBaLaHap. The HukBaLaHap, or Hukbong Bayan Laban sa Hapon, was established as a guerilla movement during the second world war. But when the American forces came back after the war, instead of recognizing the Huk's role in the Japanese resistance, they were instead forced to be exiled by the Americans, who at the time governed the country. This resulted in the persecution of Luis Taruc and other leaders. After they were granted amnesty by President Sergio Osmeña, the HukBaLaHap was formally disbanded by the Partido Komunista sa Pilipinas. However, when Osmeña lost to Roxas during the 1946 presidential election, six of their members who were supposed to have a position in the congress, including Taruc, were not allowed to earn the seats because they allegedly cheated during the campaign. Because of this, the Parity Rights Amendment by Roxas, of the 1935 Constitution was easily passed. In line with the Bell Trade Act, such an amendment shall give fair and just rights to the Filipinos and Americans to utilize the natural resources of the Philippines, and that the Americans can import without tariff. Because of this, the group was reorganized and the Huk Rebellion rose against the government, which lasted until the era of President Elpidio Quirino. Through the leadership of Ramon Magsaysay who became the Defense Minister allegedly out of the advice of the US, Luis Taruc surrendered on May 17, 1954, and this weakened the Huk Rebellion force. Even if Taruc surrendered, the idea that America controls the Philippines was never erased.

This became more apparent because of what transpired in the Philippine government. On August 30, 1951, the Mutual Defense Treaty was signed, binding the US and the Philippines. This was one of the documents that legitimized the presence of US forces in the country. On September 8, 1954, The Southeast Asia Collective Defense Treaty, more commonly known as the Manila Pact, was signed, which started the Southeast Asia Treaty Organization or SEATO, the Southeast Asian equivalent of NATO, headed by the US as an extension to the Cold War in Southeast Asia. Worst of all, corruption persisted.

Behind all this, whatever remaining nationalist and patriotist Filipino sentiment was seen as a threat. There was no unity, and worse, it was till the foreigners that dictated the course of Philippine governance. Recto and Laurel Sr. have anticipated the need to once again dedicate such sentiments to the youth.

In Laurel's defense, for example, in choosing Noli and Fili as the primary material, he remarked that Noli Me Tangere and El Filibusterismo be read by every Filipino. "They must be learned by heart, for in their pages we see ourselves as in a looking glass, lethargy and vigor, virtue and vice. Only then would we become conscious as a people, and thus prepare ourselves for painful sacrifices that ultimately lead to self-reliance, self-respect, and freedom." Noli and Fili may contain perspectives that could affect the Catholic faith, only if taught without proper guidance, thus, the controversy.

The controversies aforementioned were the root cause why the nation strayed away from the rationale of the Rizal law. One is called to look into the important points instead of focusing on the controversies. Rizal Law presents the need to re- dedicate to the Filipinos the strife for nationalism and patriotism. The law endeavors the remembrance and commemoration of the lives of our heroes, to develop the so-called national identity. According to the law, Noli and Fili can permanently plant the seed of nationalism and patriotism that needed to be cultivated in the minds of the young, and that the lessons be meaningful enough to form the so-called civic conscience. In such a way, it is up to the educational system to teach the essence of being a citizen of a nation. It may not be that important whether one is in favor of Rizal or not, or if one agrees with the mandatory reading of the Noli and Fili. One could actually appreciate the Rizal Law more if they are grounded with its rationale and implication.

# **NATIONAL HEROES: HOW ARE THEY "SELECTED"?**

Among the things one can learn about the Rizal law is that the life and works of those who lived and died for the nation are worthy of remembrance. There are people who made huge contributions to the long sought-after nationalism and patriotism, and they are what we know of as heroes.

But, in using the term "hero," another controversy that would level with or even go beyond the Rizal Law was the selection of national heroes.

Back in 2016, the former president Ferdinand Marcos was buried at the Libingan ng mga Bayani. Many expressed their dissent and anger. The reason why many were disappointed was that Marcos was not allegedly worthy of being considered a hero, because of the atrocities he committed during his dictatorship.

What then, is the basis for acknowledging someone as a hero? There might be a lot of heroes who are not worth the title, according to some. Even past Marcos, there are also those who would never consider Ninoy Aquino as a hero. Another great example is the lukewarm treatment of Aguinaldo's heroism. Even Rizal was not marked safe with this controversy. Until now, there are those who believe that Rizal was only made a hero by the US.

To answer the question, it might be necessary to trace the narrative of Rizal's heroism.

Back when Rizal was alive, he was considered a leader by the Filipinos. He was made editor of La Solidaridad, to which he declined. When the circle was looking for a head, he was chosen over Marcelo del Pilar, to which Rizal also declined. Even with such defeat, del Pilar still made effort into resolving his conflict with Rizal. This only shows the respect and honor the former had for the latter. But when La Liga Filipina, established by del Pilar after his exile, was split, the revolutionary members of the Katipunan still acknowledged Rizal as the honorary president. Bonifacio even sent an emissary to consult Rizal regarding the plot for revolt. The death of Rizal became the primary reason why those who longed for assimilation, like Mabini, finally longed for ultimate liberation. Even after his death, the Filipinos' huge honor and respect to Rizal endured. On December 20, 1898, Aguinaldo declared December 30\) as Rizal Day and National Day of Mourning. Such a date became an annual national holiday through Act No. 345 of the 1902 Philippine Commission. A lot of other laws honoring Rizal came into surface, like the Act No. 317 of 1901 that birthed the province of Rizal, Act No. 233 of 1901 that erected the Rizal monument at Luneta, RA 229 of 1948 that prohibited gambling like cockfighting every December 30, Executive Order No. 429 of President Carlos Garcia who established the Rizal Pro Patria Award, and of course the RA 1425\. Aside from these laws, a lot of places and streets all over the country were named after Rizal.

This goes to show the high regards the country gave Rizal, that which indirectly became the blatant acknowledgment of Rizal as THE national hero, superseding all other personalities one might have had encountered on postcards and posters back in elementary days which carried the label heroes.

Frankly, at first, the heroes only became heroes because of acclamation, or by virtue of popular approval. Acclamation is based upon a person's contribution to the expansion of the Filipino consciousness, and to independence. There is no law that proclaimed them national heroes. They were only coined such because a lot of Filipinos acknowledge them as heroes.

What is great about this is that the recognition came from the people; meaning, their heroism resonated with the masses. Even after the declaration of Aguinaldo that December 30 be the National Day of Mourning, people could have ignored it had they not considered Rizal a hero in the first place. What is problematic, however, is that there are also some personalities considered as heroes, who may not be worthy. This is the problem of subjectivity.

In 1993, President Fidel Ramos issued an executive order regarding the formal acknowledgment, through declaration, of national heroes. This is the Executive Order No. 75 of 1993\. The National Heroes Committee was created, composed of the Secretary of Education (Chairman), Secretary of Foreign Affairs, and Secretary of National Defense, which became the in-charge of learning about, evaluating, and recommending people subjected for the recognition and declaration of being national heroes. Because such a task was laborious, the National Historical Institute, through its Executive Director, was delegated to draw up clear-cut criteria for heroes. In 1965, another analogous commission finalized the criteria, from which Teodoro Agoncillo, a nationalist historian, was cited as an authority. To wit:

1. The extent of a person's sacrifices for the welfare of the country.
2. Motive and methods employed in the attainment of the ideal (welfare of the country). In the attainment of the ideal, did the person concerned sacrifice purely and exclusively for the welfare of the country or was there any selfish or ulterior motive in making such sacrifices? Were the methods employed in the attainment of the ideal morally valid?
3. Moral character of the person concerned. Did he do anything immoral to attain his personal character? If there was any immorality, how far did it affect his work for the society or the ideal?
4. The influence of the person concerned on his age and or the succeeding age.

The commission, however, came up with an elaboration of it later on:

"A hero is an admirable leader towering over his peers, serving a noble cause with the sword or the pen, possessing exceptional talent, distinguished in valor and/ or bold enterprise, exercising a determinative influence over the event; who in times of extreme stress and difficulties, project himself by his own fortitude, efforts, and sacrifices to be the beacon of light that guides his oppressed countrymen to their rightful destiny, and in complete self-denial abandons his personal interests to place those of country above and before any other; and whose deeds and acts are proudly emulated by a grateful people, that after his death, render him singular tributes, honor him by public worship, and acknowledge his meritorious services to mankind by spontaneous national recognition."

For Ambeth Ocampo (2009), however, identifying heroes was "difficult and sometimes politically-motivated."

The primary purpose of these criteria was to give objective parameters in the declaration of an individual as a hero, and not to remove the title from the existing heroes. But then again, no person was declared National Hero. The heroism of those we acknowledge now is only out of acclamation, and not proclamation. The question of how they were made heroes repeats, and the answer to this would be: through acclamation, before the executive order issued by Ramos, and through proclamation, based on the criteria given by the National Heroes Committee.

It is important to remember that the reason why one needs to learn about the lives and works of those who came before us is not because they are interesting, but because they are worth emulating, especially their deeds and their great cause.

# **LITERATURE AND HISTORY: A DYNAMIC RELATIONSHIP**

In the defense of Laurel on the mandatory reading of Noli and Fili, he mentioned that the Filipinos need to read them because they reflect much of whom we were and now are, by looking back to the context and to whatever transpired in the writing of and in the novels' plots.

What then, is the role of literature in molding history? In Morris West's book, The Shoes of the Fisherman, the Pope told the Chairman of the Communist Party of China and the Premier of the Soviet Union, “Mr. Chairman, what is your revolution built on? What is Russia's revolution built on? One man who spent most of his life in the British Museum is buried in a tiny cemetery in England. Karl Marx never carried a gun; he has never fought on the barricades. All he had was words, words and an idea, which in 60 years has changed the face of the earth."

Two presuppositions illustrate the equal importance of history and literature. First, literature merits the truth-telling of history. Second, literature has the stronghold in presenting history as truthful and relevant.

Even in the existence of a lot of historical literature besides history books, still there are many historical inaccuracies, which may obscure the teaching and learning of a nation's culture and history. It is in this way that literature helps validate the identity of a nation, and in turn, mold the concept of nationalism.

The Alexander Dumas classic, "The Three Musketeers" narrates the story of Athos, Porthos, and Aramis, including D'Artagnan. Even if a portion of the novel was inaccurate once viewed in the historical lens, the whole novel tells of the internal conflict within France, amidst its conflict with the Protestant England. The characters were allegedly based on real people, such as the cousins Armand d'Athos, Isaac de Porthau, and Henri d'Aramitz who were Gascony noblemen, while d'Artagnan was based on Count Charles Ogier de Batz de Castelmore. Cardinal Richelieu was based on Cardinal Armand Jean du Plessis, Duke of Richelieu.

Besides The Three Musketeers, the plot of the novel, A Tale of Two Cities by Charles Dickens was based on true events, though it was not confirmed whether the characters were based on real persons. The novel revolved around London and Paris during the French Revolution. The presentation of Dickens on the overthrowing of aristocracy and on the gruesome atrocities during the Reign of Terror was so close to what really transpired, if not entirely similar, during that era.

These literary works are not only popular, but also significant in cultivating the aspired nationalism of France and England.

Rizal's Noli Me Tangere and El Filibusterismo were placed side by side with the literary classics aforementioned. Just like The Three Musketeers, many characters of the novels were based upon real persons. Similar with the song of Francisco Baltazar in Florante at Laura, the pain and desolation felt by Florante for his "bayan kong sawi", and regarding Laura whom he loved, both of which were taken away and ruled by an impostor, captures the imagery of the pain and suffering of the natives from the lands that fell into the hands of the colonizers. Just like the imprisonment of Bernardo in the Alamat ni Bernardo Carpio, accused of a crime he did not know he committed, depicted the abuse experienced in the hands of the Spaniards, while his coming escape shows the hope that tomorrow promises liberation and redemption.

With regard to literature about culture, there is Indarapatra at Sulayman. The prowess of Sulayman and Indarapatra in slaying the beast shows the typical chivalry of the natives even in the midst of an impossible adversary.

The Dasalan at Tocsohan of Marcelo del Pilar also helped awaken people from the duality of the friars. Even those who are foreign to the masterpiece, Uncle Tom's Cabin of Harriet Beecher Stowe, and The Count of Monte Cristo of Alexander Dumas, could say that literature has played an important role in shaping history, as these classics greatly influenced Rizal, whose novels later on carried similar themes. Besides illustrating and even predicting history, literature has the power to shift its course. Words are just as powerful as any other sharp weapon.
